# EFSA Standard-Adult Reference Review

## Scope and disposition

This is the independent second source review of the 15 EFSA rows listed in `IMPLEMENTATION_ROADMAP.md` §8.5 (Q27–Q40). It checks the adopted opinions as primary sources, not secondary nutrition tables. It does not provide clinical validation and does not activate any reference in the application. User-entered/manual targets remain independent of this review.

**Approved** means the full adopted opinion was reviewed and supports the mapped reference, unit, type, and stated cohort. **Conditional / deactivated** means the published original abstract supports the explicit headline value/type (and cohort only where stated), but the adopted article body was not available for review. Those rows must remain inactive; do not infer age limits, sex coverage, health status, or other conditions not explicit in the abstract. Revisit them only after full-opinion review.

All approved values below are source-specific dietary reference values, not individualized medical advice. The roadmap’s healthy-adult, non-pregnant/non-lactating context is not extended to a source subgroup unless the evidence below states it. Source location is given as the original article’s abstract and relevant section/page; “adopted” dates are included where confirmed, with first publication date as the version identifier.

## Row decisions

1. **Q27 — Protein — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2012;10(2):2557, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2557); adopted 2012-01-19, first published 2012-02-09. Full adopted article: [PDF hosted on the University of Naples Federico II faculty site](http://wpage.unina.it/fogliano/Slides%20FIPDes%20Seminars/EFSA%20OPINION%20DRV%20PROTEIN%2026-09-11.pdf), publisher article text (not a consultation draft); abstract p. 1, summary pp. 2–3 and §6.1 “Protein requirement of adults,” p. 30. The opinion sets an adult PRI of **0.83 g protein/kg body weight/day**, applicable to adults of both sexes and all adult ages; it is based on nitrogen balance and high-quality protein (the opinion also discusses mixed diets). Preserve the per-kilogram denominator. Do not produce a daily gram target without an explicit, justified body-weight input.

2. **Q28 — Available carbohydrate — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2010;8(3):1462, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1462); adopted 2009-12-04, first published 2010-03-25. Source location: abstract p. 1; §6.1, p. 35. Adult RI: **45–60% of total energy** for available/glycaemic carbohydrate. Preserve the energy-percentage range; do not convert to grams unless a planning-energy value is explicitly selected.

3. **Q28 — Dietary fibre — APPROVED.** Same opinion and dates as row 2. Source location: abstract p. 1; §6.3, p. 36. Adult AI: **25 g/day**, based on normal laxation. This is the adult value, not the separate child rule expressed per unit of energy.

4. **Q29 — Total fat — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2010;8(3):1461, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1461); adopted 2009-12-04, first published 2010-03-25. Source location: abstract p. 1; summary p. 2. Adult RI range: **20–35% of total energy**. Preserve E%; no gram amount without an explicitly selected energy basis.

5. **Q30 — Vitamin A — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2015;13(3):4028, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4028); adopted 2015-02-05, first published 2015-03-05. Source text: publisher’s PDF/version of record in [DTU Orbit](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/118572415/4028.pdf); abstract p. 1, §6.1 p. 38 and Table 5 p. 39. The opinion explicitly derives the PRI for **healthy adults**: **750 µg RE/day for men; 650 µg RE/day for women**. Its reference body weights are based on measured EU adults aged 18–79; that sample description is not a 79-year applicability limit. The opinion gives separate pregnancy/lactation values. Preserve retinol equivalents (RE), not RAE.

6. **Q31 — Vitamin D — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2016;14(10):4547, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4547); adopted 2016-06-29, first published 2016-10-28. Source text: publisher’s PDF/version of record in the [University of Copenhagen repository](https://curis.ku.dk/ws/files/188394630/EFSA_Panel_on_Dietetic_Products_Nutrition_and_Allergies_NDA_Sjodin_EFSA_Journal_2016_Vol_14_10_4547.pdf); abstract p. 1, summary pp. 3–6 and §6.1 p. 73. Adult AI: **15 µg/day**; the opinion could not derive an AR/PRI. The meta-regression assumes **minimal cutaneous vitamin D synthesis**; with cutaneous synthesis, dietary need may be lower or zero. The source uses the adult population without a more specific age cutoff in this row; it separately states that pregnant and lactating women receive the same AI.

7. **Q32 — Vitamin E — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2015;13(7):4149, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4149); adopted 2015-06-11, first published 2015-07-09. Source location: abstract p. 1; summary and §6. Adult AI: **13 mg/day for men; 11 mg/day for women**, expressed as **α-tocopherol**. These are AI values based on observed intake in healthy European populations, not an AR/PRI; do not relabel the source form as α-tocopherol equivalents.

8. **Q33 — Vitamin K — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2017;15(5):e04780, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4780); adopted 2017-04-05, first published 2017-05-22. Source location: abstract p. 1; summary. Adult AI: **70 µg/day phylloquinone (vitamin K1)**, derived from 1 µg/kg/day using the adult reference weight. The opinion does not support representing this as total vitamin K or as a K2/menaquinone reference.

9. **Q34 — Vitamin B6 — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2016;14(6):e04485, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4485); first published 2016-06-24. Full article: [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11847984/) ([PDF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11847984/pdf/EFS2-14-e04485.pdf)); publisher DOI and article metadata identify the published opinion. Source location: abstract and summary; §6.1.1 “Women” and §6.1.2 “Men” (article-number pagination). Adult PRI: **1.7 mg/day for men; 1.6 mg/day for women**. The source does not require a protein-based adjustment. If food-composition amounts are in µg, compare only after confirming the source unit and converting consistently (1 mg = 1,000 µg); do not assume the database unit.

10. **Q35 — Vitamin B12 — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2015;13(7):4150, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4150); adopted 2015-06-11, first published 2015-07-09. Source location: abstract p. 1. Adult AI: **4 µg/day**, based on status biomarkers and observed European adult intakes. No pregnancy/lactation increment is included in this adult row.

11. **Q36 — Vitamin C — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2013;11(11):3418, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2013.3418); adopted 2013-10-10, first published November 2013 (exact day not confirmed). Full publisher’s PDF/version of record: [DTU Orbit](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/60085742/vitamin%20C.pdf). Source location: abstract p. 1; summary pp. 2–3; §6.1 “Adults,” pp. 30–31. Adult PRI: **110 mg/day for men; 95 mg/day for women**, derived using a 10% CV. The opinion does not establish a separate older-adult value.

12. **Q37 — Folate — CONDITIONAL / DEACTIVATED.** EFSA Journal 2014;12(11):3893, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3893); adopted 2014-10-30, first published 2014-11-20 (record updated 2015-02-06). The published abstract supports the adult **PRI of 330 µg dietary folate equivalents (DFE)/day** and states the associated adult AR (250 µg DFE/day), status biomarkers, and 15% CV rationale. Preserve DFE; do not substitute a chemical folic-acid amount. Full adopted text remains unavailable through the official/institutional routes tried; keep inactive pending full-source review of the additional cohort and dietary-basis conditions.

13. **Q38 — Calcium — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2015;13(5):4101, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4101); adopted 2015-04-23, first published 2015-05-27. Source text: publisher’s PDF/version of record in [DTU Orbit](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/118542813/4101.pdf); abstract p. 1, summary pp. 2–4, §6.3.1 p. 38 and §6.3.2 p. 39. Adult PRI: **1,000 mg/day at ages 18–24; 950 mg/day from age 25**. The opinion does not distinguish these adult values by sex. It says the non-pregnant woman’s PRI also applies in pregnancy/lactation for the same age group; the roadmap baseline remains the non-pregnant context.

14. **Q39 — Magnesium — APPROVED.** EFSA Journal 2015;13(7):4186, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4186); adopted 2015-06-29, first published 2015-07-27. Source location: abstract pp. 1–3; §6.1, p. 28. Adult AI: **350 mg/day for men; 300 mg/day for women**. The values are based on observed intakes in healthy European populations; do not relabel them as PRI values.

15. **Q40 — Sodium — APPROVED AS A DISTINCT CATEGORY.** EFSA Journal 2019;17(9):5778, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2019.5778); adopted 2019-07-03, first published 2019-09-04. Source location: abstract p. 1; summary pp. 3–5; §6.1.3, p. 47. Adult **safe and adequate intake: 2.0 g sodium/day**. EFSA did not establish an AR/PRI from the available evidence. This is **not** an AI, a minimum intake target, or a UL; retain a separate category and never show it as an individualized minimum.

## Source-access limits

The full adopted opinions for Q30, Q31 and Q38 were initially unavailable from the publisher route, but their publisher-version-of-record PDFs were obtained from DTU Orbit (Q30/Q38) and the University of Copenhagen repository (Q31), allowing full review and closing those gates. Q37 remains conditional/deactivated. Attempts for its final article: the EFSA publication page (`https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3893`) and publisher article route (`https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3893`) resolve to a publisher response that returns HTTP 403; the corresponding historical EFSA PDF asset path (`https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/scientific_output/files/main_documents/3893.pdf`) also redirects to that 403 response. The official EFSA consultation PDF (`https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/consultation/140722.pdf`) is a pre-adoption draft, not the adopted article, and was not used as a substitute. The University of Helsinki record (`https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/scientific-opinion-on-dietary-reference-values-for-folate/`) and Finnish Research.fi record (`https://research.fi/en/results/publication/0010431814`) provide bibliographic metadata but no deposited full text; OpenAIRE’s [DOI record](https://api.openaire.eu/search/publications?doi=10.2903%2Fj.efsa.2014.3893&format=json) lists metadata sources without an alternative full-text repository. A candidate INSA repository URL surfaced in search but failed certificate verification before its document identity could be confirmed (`https://repositorio.insa.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d574e3-b755-4b54-aa9e-6b9b890fa6f6/content`), so it was not relied on. Search also surfaced a third-party mirror whose author/institutional provenance and posting rights could not be verified; it was not accessed or used. Q37 therefore remains inactive until a lawful full adopted opinion copy is available. Its original abstract supports the headline PRI only and does not justify additional inferred assumptions.

This review records attributed numerical reference facts and source metadata; it does not reproduce EFSA prose, tables, or figures, and it makes no blanket claim to permission to adapt or republish article content. Preserve source/DOI attribution and check each article’s own notice before redistributing any compiled reference data.
